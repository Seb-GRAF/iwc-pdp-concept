      41 mm across

      A stainless steel case sized between the Mark XX and the Big Pilot’s Watch 43, with the pushers at 2 and 4 o’clock and a screw-in crown between them.

    2. Sheet 2 of 3 · Side elevation

      14.5 mm high

      Convex sapphire glass on top, a sapphire case back below. The glass is secured against displacement by a drop in air pressure, and the case is water-resistant to 10 bar.

      242 components

      The IWC-manufactured calibre 69385 winds itself, beats at 4 Hz on 33 jewels and runs for 46 hours once fully wound.

        Technical data

        Chronograph

        Time anything up to 12 hours

        The pusher at 2 o’clock starts and stops the chronograph, the one at 4 o’clock resets it. Elapsed seconds run on the centre hand, minutes on the counter at 12 o’clock and hours on the counter at 9 o’clock.

        00h : 00min : 00.000s

        Press Space to start or stop, R to reset.

        Stopped · 8 steps per second

        EasX-CHANGE®

        Change the strap in seconds

        Press the button on the underside of the strap to release it, then click in the next one. No tools needed.

        About the collection

        Pilot’s Watches

        IWC has built watches for the cockpit since 1936. The brief has hardly changed: read the time at a glance, in any light, and keep running through cold, vibration and magnetic fields. Today the collection runs from the Mark XX to the Big Pilot’s Watch and its complications.

        Discover the collection
        1. 1936

          The Special Pilot’s Watch, IWC’s first, with a rotating bezel and an anti-magnetic escapement.

        2. 1940

          The Big Pilot’s Watch: a 55 mm case and a crown large enough to turn with gloves on.

        3. 1948

          The Mark 11 for the Royal Air Force, with a soft-iron inner cage against magnetic fields.

        4. 2021

          The Pilot’s Watch Chronograph 41, with the IWC-manufactured calibre 69385 and EasX-CHANGE straps.
